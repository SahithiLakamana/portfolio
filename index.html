Automated Demographic Extraction with LLM-as-a-Judge Validation

Hybrid human-LLM pipeline extracting self-reported age and gender from 2.3M social media posts, revealing critical risks in LLM-as-a-judge methodology through multi-model agreement analysis.

LLMsPrompt EngineeringModel EvaluationValidation FrameworkMulti-Model Agreement
  • Designed two-stage annotation combining human labeling (κ = 0.95 age, κ = 1.00 gender) with parallel LLM extraction across three models (Llama 3 70B, GPT-OSS 120B, Gemini Gemma 3 27B) on 4,328 users and 2.3M posts.
  • Built LLM-as-a-judge validation with Gemini 3 27B, but identified critical bias: self-validation confirmation rates diverged 10.6x (4.0% vs 42.7% for Llama), revealing methodological risk of same-model extraction + validation.
  • Achieved F1 ≈ 0.93 (age) and F1 ≈ 0.90 (gender) on ensemble majority-vote; gender showed 98.9% inter-model consistency (1 disagreement across 856 users) vs. only 39% for age.
  • Revealed speed-accuracy trade-off: Gemini Gemma 3 at 1.3 sec/user (4,151 outputs) vs. Llama 3 70B at 37 sec/user with 42.7% validation confirmation; GPT-OSS 120B's 0.79 precision on gender most reliable.

Research Design

Two-stage process: human annotators labeled explicit age/gender disclosures (125 age, 92 gender posts as ground truth); parallel LLM extraction using structured prompts restricting outputs to explicitly self-stated values. Inverse probability weighting addressed selection bias (1,225 age, 513 gender, 430 both vs. 4,328 total users).

Prompt Engineering

Structured prompt enforced strict extraction constraints: extract ONLY explicitly self-stated demographics, ignore other people's mentions, do not assume or guess, capture experience-based age references. Output format: "age | gender" with "null" for missing values. Validation instructions: return "yes", "no", or "cannot be determined" for extracted values.

LLM-as-a-Judge Validation — Critical Finding

Gemini 3 27B validated whether extracted demographics were explicitly self-reported in original posts. Results revealed a major methodological risk:

Model Being ValidatedConfirmation RateRejection RateRate Difference vs. Llama
Llama 3 70B1,450 (42.7%)1,563 (46.0%)—
GPT-OSS 120B461 (30.1%)910 (59.4%)1.4x
Gemini 3 27B (self-validation)169 (4.0%)3,686 (86.9%)10.6x

The problem: Using the same model for both extraction and validation creates circular bias. Gemini rejected its own outputs 86.9% of the time, while independent Llama validation on those same extractions showed 42.7% confirmation. On gold-standard human annotations (103 posts), all models achieved identical F1 = 0.93, proving the validation discrepancy reflects methodology, not quality.

Agreement Analysis

Gender: 308/856 identical outputs (36%); 98.9% consistency across all three models (only 1 complete disagreement among 856 users).
Age: 48/123 full agreement (39%); 43/123 complete disagreement (35%).

Error distribution: GPT-OSS 120B most precise (5 age, 82 gender); Llama 3 70B highest volume (3,331 age, 2,762 gender); Gemini 3 27B tied with GPT-OSS on age accuracy (5 errors) but higher gender errors (231).

Data Processing & Statistical Control

  • Temporal filtering: For users with multiple age disclosures, selected closest to T0, adjusted forward using elapsed time; excluded 31 users (2.5%) with inconsistent ages.
  • Age categorization: Four bands (<20, 20–29, 30–39, 40+).
  • Selection bias: Applied inverse probability weighting to account for non-random sampling of demographic subsets.
  • Validation methodology: Independent validation on 103 human-labeled posts to avoid circular bias.
